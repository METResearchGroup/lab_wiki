## Working hours

- Working hours are 10–5, M–F, with the exception of holidays.
- If you are going on vacation, please let me know in advance.

## Communication

- Communication happens over email and Slack. Check both at least once per day during working hours.
- **Email** is typically for bigger things — requesting feedback on an entire manuscript, for example.
- **Slack** is typically for small things — advice on X, input before launching a study, a data analysis strategy.
- There is no expectation that you or I respond to messages outside of working hours.

## Working with me

- **No-guilt policy:** let's do our best to create a working relationship where guilt is rare. For me, this mostly means open communication:
  - Admit mistakes.
  - Discuss concerns or problems early.
  - If you don't know, ask.
- My main interest is to put you in a position to succeed, where success is defined by your goals. I understand that goals shift over time, and I'm happy to discuss broad questions anytime.
- **Open-door policy:** on days when I'm on campus, feel free to drop in whenever you want to discuss what's on your mind. You can also Slack me to set up an impromptu Zoom meeting if I'm not on campus.

## Meetings

- Core lab members have regular weekly or bi-weekly meetings unless otherwise specified.
- Come prepared with the content discussed in the previous meeting, as written in the weekly goals doc.
- If you don't have anything prepared or ready that week — this should be rare — please email me to cancel, and explain why. That frees up the meeting time.

## Feedback

- For anything needing substantive feedback (manuscript, talk, project proposal), plan for a **two-week lead time**.
- I typically turn feedback around faster than that, but build your timelines around the worst case.
- In the unlikely event that it's been longer than two weeks, email me a reminder. This is helpful rather than annoying.

## Lab culture

- Aim to be as collaborative as possible: share resources, peer-mentor, and collaborate on projects when appropriate.
- Foster a culture of respect and enthusiasm. We should feel comfortable giving honest feedback, and also shining a light on the positive elements of each other's work.

## Responsible use of AI

Generative AI is now part of how research gets done, and I'd rather we be deliberate about it than pretend otherwise. The goal is to use these tools without hollowing out the skills that make you a researcher.

### Principles

- **Use GenAI in proportion to how well you already know something.** The temptation to lean on it will be strongest exactly where you find the work hardest — which is exactly where the learning is. It will also happily feed your productive-procrastination habits. Fight that.
- **Know which parts of your work are the most critical.** Can you defend a choice, state what would falsify your result, or explain why this analysis and not another one? Those decisions need to be yours.
- **These tools are sycophantic.** They will tell you what you want to hear and won't reliably tell you when you're wrong. Agreement from a model is not evidence that you're right.
- **Quantity is not the goal.** Hiring committees are starting to ask what you can do that a chatbot subscription cannot. If the honest answer is "produce more of the same," that's a problem.
- **Ask others to help hold you accountable.** Your mentors and peers can help keep you honest about how you're using these tools. Use them for that.
- **What counts as a paper is about to change.** Optimizing for today's publishable unit means being permanently a step behind. Learn your domain well enough to lead rather than follow.

### Practical rules

- Don't upload any identifiable data into third-party AI tools.
- Disclose AI use as required by the journal, conference, or funder. Assume you will need to describe what you used and how.
- AI tools are not authors and cannot be credited under [CRediT](https://credit.niso.org/). You are accountable for everything that goes out under your name, including anything a model got wrong.
- If you're unsure whether a particular use is appropriate, ask me. I'd much rather have that conversation early.

## Authorship

- For PhD students and postdocs, if you are leading the work you will be first author and I will be senior (last) author. Leading includes ideation, performing the research, data analysis, and writing.
- For PhD students, I will be listed as corresponding author. For postdocs or PhD students actively on the job market, we will be shared corresponding authors.
- Authorship can be a grey area and sometimes requires negotiation. This conversation should happen early — ideally at the very start of each project — to avoid awkward situations later. The same goes for deciding whether collaborators or other students will be authors.
- We use the [CRediT](https://credit.niso.org/) system for recognizing contributions to a paper.

## Collaborations

If you are my primary mentee and not co-advised, it's still fine to have an independent project with another PI, under these conditions:

- Your collaboration is openly communicated so that I'm aware of it.
- You are prioritizing our main project together, and progress is consistent.
- You are paying attention to burnout. It's very easy for students to get stretched thin through overcommitment.

## Conferences

- If you are presenting one of our primary projects at a conference, I can typically fund your flight and/or registration fees. A "primary project" means you are lead author and I'm senior author.
- Kellogg PhD students have $4,000 for conference travel, so I would fund you only after you've used that.
- There are also two travel grants for grad students:
  - [TGS conference travel grant](https://www.tgs.northwestern.edu/funding/fellowships-and-grants/internal-fellowships-grants/conference-travel-grant.html)
  - [Buffett Institute travel grant](https://buffett.northwestern.edu/programs/graduate-opportunities/graduate-development-funding/conference-travel-award/)
- Conference attendance and travel need to be approved by me before submission.
